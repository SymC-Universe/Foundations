It does not claim to be an exhaustive systematic review. `NO_FULL_SEQUENCE_PRECEDENT_LOCATED` means only that the targeted searches recorded here did not locate a study matching the full SI evidentiary sequence.

The target sequence remains:

1. independently characterize the parent/substrate architecture;
2. freeze the physical carrier/subspace correspondence before target inspection;
3. prospectively predict the child/coupled carrier transformation;
4. compare against an independently generated child object;
5. intervene on the parent and test the predicted child change;
6. defeat frequency-only, same-spectrum, generic-parent, or coupling-specificity controls;
7. preserve uncertainty and nonidentifiability;
8. when a reduced subsystem is reused, test the declared higher-level quantities after re-embedding.

Finding prior work that already performs some or all of this sequence is a positive scientific outcome because it narrows what SI can legitimately claim.

## 1. Adsorbate vibration mode tracking is established more directly than the v0.1 collision captured

### Herrmann and Reiher (2006)

Carmen Herrmann and Markus Reiher, **“Direct targeting of adsorbate vibrations with mode-tracking,”** *Surface Science* 600, 1891-1900 (2006). DOI `10.1016/j.susc.2006.01.054`.

The study directly targets adsorbate normal modes for thiophenolate on Ag(111) using a mode-tracking protocol and compares the targeted modes with full frequency analysis across related structural treatments.

### SI consequence

This strengthens the prior-art ceiling around any claim that SI introduces the idea of tracking physically relevant adsorbate vibrational carriers. It does not by itself appear to implement the full parent-clean-surface -> prospectively frozen parent/child map -> independent child reveal -> parent intervention -> specificity-control sequence.

Disposition:

`ADSORBATE_MODE_TRACKING = ESTABLISHED_NATIVE_METHOD`

`FULL_SI_SEQUENCE = NOT_DEMONSTRATED_BY_THIS_PRECEDENT`

## 2. Clean versus adsorbate-covered surface phonon comparison is established

### Surface phonon dispersion of ordered overlayers (1986)

**“Surface phonon dispersion of ordered overlayers,”** *Journal of Electron Spectroscopy and Related Phenomena* 38, 29-44 (1986). DOI `10.1016/0368-2048(86)85070-8`.

The work compares surface phonons and surface resonances of clean and adsorbate-covered Ni(100), including ordered sulfur, oxygen, and carbon overlayers and random oxygen adsorption, using high-resolution electron energy-loss spectroscopy.

### Fritsch, Arnold, and Schröder (2000)

J. Fritsch, M. Arnold, and U. Schröder, **“Ab initio calculation of the phonon dispersion of antimony-covered (110) surfaces of III-V compounds,”** *Physical Review B* 61, 16682 (2000). DOI `10.1103/PhysRevB.61.16682`.

The calculations explicitly identify overlayer vibrations, interface modes, and phonons related to pristine-surface modes whose dispersion changes after adsorption, with comparisons across several III-V surfaces.

### SI consequence

The broad question “can clean-surface and adsorbate-covered vibrational structures be compared and related?” is clearly prior art. The residual SI question must therefore remain prospective and evidentiary rather than descriptive. SI needs to predefine which parent carriers should survive or transform and then test that prediction under intervention and specificity controls.

Disposition:

`CLEAN_VS_COVERED_PHONON_COMPARISON = ESTABLISHED`

`DESCRIPTIVE_MODE_RELATEDNESS = NOT_SI_NOVELTY`

## 3. Uncertainty propagation from components to assembled-system response is established

### Hinke et al. (2009)

L. Hinke, F. Dohnal, B. R. Mace, T. P. Waters, and N. Ferguson, **“Component mode synthesis as a framework for uncertainty analysis,”** *Journal of Sound and Vibration* 324, 161-178 (2009). DOI `10.1016/j.jsv.2009.01.056`.

The paper treats CMS as a framework for introducing and propagating component-level uncertainty into assembled-system dynamics, including uncertainty in component physical and modal properties.

### Chatterjee, Adhikari, and Friswell (2020)

T. Chatterjee, S. Adhikari, and M. I. Friswell, **“Uncertainty propagation in dynamic sub-structuring by model reduction integrated domain decomposition,”** *Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering* 366, 113060 (2020). DOI `10.1016/j.cma.2020.113060`.

The work propagates uncertainty from subcomponents through a reduced dynamic-substructuring framework to system-level response.

### SI consequence

SI cannot claim novelty for propagating subsystem uncertainty into a coupled response. The candidate residual contribution is narrower: preserving uncertainty as part of a prospective parent-to-child carrier correspondence and refusing inheritance when the claimed carrier identity is not identifiable against the uncertainty envelope.

Disposition:

`COMPONENT_TO_SYSTEM_UNCERTAINTY_PROPAGATION = ESTABLISHED_NATIVE_METHOD`

## 4. Hierarchical transfer-path tracing is now a particularly close neighboring method family

### El Kadmiri Pedraza, Monner, and Algermissen (2026)

S. El Kadmiri Pedraza, H. P. Monner, and S. Algermissen, **“A framework for simulation-based transfer path analysis using dynamic substructuring and component mode synthesis,”** *Computers & Structures* 321, 108097 (2026). DOI `10.1016/j.compstruc.2026.108097`.

The paper integrates Dynamic Substructuring and Component Mode Synthesis with simulation-based Transfer Path Analysis and extends the construction to multilevel TPA, tracing force/displacement contributions across hierarchical subsystem levels.

This is especially relevant to SI because it explicitly treats hierarchical vibration transmission through interconnected subsystem levels. It is therefore a stronger neighboring comparator than generic CMS alone for future SI tasks involving perturbation propagation or source-to-receiver contribution paths.

### SI consequence

SI may not claim novelty merely for tracing contributions through multiple hierarchy levels. The residual distinction, if it survives future comparison, is the prospective inheritance evidence sequence: parent carrier architecture is independently characterized and used to predict which child carrier/subspace structures survive or transform, with frozen mapping, intervention, specificity controls, refusal, and a separate test of higher-level quantity preservation.

Disposition:

`MULTILEVEL_TRANSFER_PATH_TRACING = ESTABLISHED/ACTIVE_NATIVE_METHOD_FAMILY`

`FUTURE_COMPARATOR_CANDIDATE = YES_FOR_HIERARCHICAL_RESPONSE_TASKS`

## 6. What the deeper collision changes

The v0.2 collision materially narrows four areas.

### 6.1 Mode tracking

Mode tracking is not merely established in structural dynamics; it has been used specifically to target adsorbate vibrational modes on surfaces. Any SI contribution must begin after that method primitive.

### 6.2 Parent/covered-system comparison

Clean-versus-adsorbate-covered surface-phonon comparison is established experimentally and computationally. SI cannot claim novelty for associating covered-surface modes with structures of the pristine surface.

### 6.3 Uncertainty

Component-level uncertainty propagation into assembled-system dynamics is established. SI's possible residual contribution is the role of uncertainty in a fail-closed **inheritance claim**, not uncertainty propagation itself.

### 6.4 Hierarchical response/path analysis

Modern DS/CMS and transfer-path methods explicitly analyze multilevel transmission through coupled systems. SI's hierarchical question must therefore be tied to prospectively declared carrier inheritance and quantity-specific closure, not generic hierarchical propagation.
