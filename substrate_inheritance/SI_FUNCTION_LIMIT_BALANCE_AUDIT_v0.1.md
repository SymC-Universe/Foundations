# Substrate Inheritance Function/Limit balance audit v0.1

**Date:** 14 September 2026  
**Scope:** completed SI P0-D/P0-Q evidence and the living manuscript  
**Purpose:** ensure ordinary functioning behavior, limits, negative results, and refusals are represented proportionally rather than allowing either success cases or dramatic failures to dominate the scientific story.

## Executive verdict

The current SI evidence base is unusually well suited to a balanced Function/Limit presentation because FM1-FM4 were explicitly designed as landscapes rather than binary gate hunts. The main manuscript risk is no longer missing limit information. It is **narrative imbalance**: the failure cases are memorable, while broad functioning interiors can disappear into tables. The living manuscript should therefore pair each major limit with the functioning regime from which that limit emerges.

No omnibus Function/Limit score is recommended.

## 1. FM1 coupling response

### Function Map

- zero coupling behaves as a proper null;
- nonzero coupling produces graded embedded response and intervention sensitivity;
- carrier assignment remains strong over substantial parts of the geometry grid;
- substrate participation stays high across much of the frozen surface;
- coupling rewires produce measurable response differences over ordinary nonzero coupling.

### Limit Map

- carrier correspondence weakens with increasing geometry mismatch;
- frequency-only assignment can become materially worse than carrier-resolved assignment;
- rewire sensitivity is not globally monotonic;
- response magnitude, intervention sensitivity, participation, and correspondence cannot be treated as interchangeable.

### Manuscript balance rule

Do not present FM1 merely as “frequency matching fails.” The stronger result is that **there is a graded functioning interior in which several relational quantities vary differently**, and frequency-only failure emerges as one boundary of that interior.

## 2. FM2 embedding depth

### Function Map

- finite-depth embedding approaches the analytic semi-infinite reference across the declared grid;
- all 48 cases eventually reach every predeclared numerical task tolerance within the listed depth set;
- shallow depths are sufficient for many ordinary cases and looser tasks.

### Limit Map

- required depth varies strongly by task/regime;
- maximum effective depth reaches 32 or 64 depending on task;
- two cases show non-monotonic error steps;
- first-crossing convergence is unsafe in those cases.

### Manuscript balance rule

Do not reduce FM2 to “depth is non-monotonic” or to “depth 64 is required.” The core result is **task-dependent reduction depth over a wide functioning surface**, with non-monotonic cases demonstrating why sustained convergence is needed.

## 3. FM3 lineage flow

### Function Map

- continuous splitting/mixing behavior is resolved over transformation strength;
- direct carrier comparison preserves exact reconvergence in the known-truth construction;
- subspace structure remains informative when individual identities degrade;
- descriptive flow quantities remain useful for local descendant-weight summaries.

### Limit Map

- exact ties correctly destroy unique descendant identifiability;
- perturbation plus spectral crowding erodes individual carrier identity;
- sequential row-normalized flow loses end-to-end reconvergence information;
- full-rank construction does not contain extinction and no extinction is manufactured.

### Manuscript balance rule

FM3 is not “the lineage method failed.” A specific compressed representation failed to preserve one class of higher-generation information, while direct carrier/subspace geometry remained correct. The result supports a **layered lineage representation**, not abandonment of lineage analysis.

## 4. FM4 hierarchical closure

### Function Map

- full, direct Schur, and nested Schur responses agree at numerical precision for the declared linear quantities;
- exact dynamic grouping therefore preserves those quantities over the frozen grid;
- both approximate methods have broad regions of small response error.

### Limit Map

- static condensation produces large errors in selected regimes;
- the one-mode approximation also fails strongly in restricted regions;
- compactness/local fit does not imply higher-level adequacy;
- no post hoc physical adequacy cutoff or method ranking is justified.

### Manuscript balance rule

Do not present FM4 only through the spectacular maximum errors. The scientific point is that **approximate closure can look excellent over much of the Function Map and still fail sharply where the higher-level task probes information the reduction did not preserve**.

## 5. Cross-map functioning structure

The combined Function Map currently supports these synthetic structural statements:

1. carrier correspondence can remain strong across ordinary coupling/geometry regimes while degrading continuously rather than at one natural threshold;
2. embedded response and intervention sensitivity grow and reorganize with coupling but are not reducible to one correspondence metric;
3. finite reductions can be highly effective when the retained information matches the task;
4. exact grouping can preserve selected higher-level responses;
5. direct carrier/subspace descriptions can remain reliable even when a compressed lineage statistic loses information;
6. no single scalar is presently shown to preserve all of these behaviors jointly.

## 6. Cross-map limits

The combined Limit Map currently establishes:

1. scalar nonidentifiability under deliberately matched response;
2. frequency-only false friends;
3. individual-mode ambiguity near degeneracy;
4. task-dependent and occasionally non-monotonic reduction convergence;
5. information loss under sequential normalized flow;
6. severe regime-local error under lossy hierarchical approximations;
7. refusal when physical input is missing;
8. no physical threshold earned from synthetic surfaces.

## 7. Figure priorities for balanced communication

### Figure candidate 1: SI evidence architecture

Show:

`parent characterization -> frozen correspondence -> child prediction -> intervention -> specificity -> inheritance disposition`

Alongside the six gates, display scalar / modal-subspace / conglomeration-system as complementary reporting channels, with uncertainty/refusal cutting across them.

Purpose: reader orientation, not result promotion.

### Figure candidate 2: Function/Limit atlas of synthetic qualification

Four-panel conceptual/result figure:

- FM1: carrier geometry × coupling strength;
- FM2: retained depth × task/regime;
- FM3: direct geometry versus normalized-flow summary;
- FM4: exact versus approximate closure.

Each panel should show an ordinary supported interior and a limit/failure region. Avoid a red/green “SI works/doesn't work” binary visual.

### Figure candidate 3: information-loss ladder

Illustrate distinct reductions that can lose different information:

`governing object -> scalar`  
`carrier sequence -> normalized flow`  
`full subsystem -> approximate reduced component`

Use actual demonstrated counterexamples to show what is lost. Do not imply that all compression is invalid.

### Figure candidate 4: physical test path

Place established native methods around the future physical experiment so the SI residual question is visually obvious. This reduces the risk that the reader interprets the manuscript as claiming invention of mode tracking, CMS, or embedding.

## 8. Primary-text prominence rules

- The abstract should contain at least one functioning result and at least one limit/negative result.
- The Results section should pair Function and Limit subsections rather than relegate all failures to limitations.
- Exact closure and approximate failure belong together.
- Direct reconvergence and normalized-flow information loss belong together.
- Effective-depth success and non-monotonic convergence belong together.
- Strong carrier overlap and frequency-only failure belong together.
- The absence of physical evidence belongs in the abstract/conclusion, not only a supplement.

## 9. Supplement versus main text

### Main text

Keep:

- six-gate architecture;
- representative FM1-FM4 Function/Limit results;
- scalar nonidentifiability;
- direct-versus-flow FM3 negative result;
- exact-versus-approximate FM4 closure;
- physical evidence boundary;
- native-method attribution.

### Supplement/reproducibility

Move detailed grids, seeds, full condition-number distributions, exact workflow metadata, exhaustive known-bad cases, and extended sensitivity tables there once a manuscript freeze exists.

The main text must retain enough quantitative information for the reader to see that negative and ordinary regimes were both tested.
