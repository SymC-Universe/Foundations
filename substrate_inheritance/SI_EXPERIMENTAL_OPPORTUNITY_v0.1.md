## Epistemic firewall

This record intentionally precedes the targeted prior-experiment search for the questions below.

It is not a novelty claim, not a statement that these experiments have not been done, and not physical evidence for substrate inheritance. The next step is to collide these independently generated designs with the literature and learn what is already answered, what methods should be inherited, and what residual question remains.

Any later literature result that already answers one of these questions is a scientific gain and should reduce or eliminate unnecessary new experimentation.

## Baseline native-science orientation

The SI question is not whether coupled systems influence one another. Coupling, hybridization, normal-mode mixing, Green-function embedding, effective Hamiltonians, self-energy, transfer functions, and subsystem reduction are established native concepts.

The narrower SI question is whether an independently characterized parent/substrate architecture can support a prospectively specified, carrier-resolved prediction about what survives, transforms, splits, mixes, disappears, or emerges in a child/coupled system, with intervention and specificity controls strong enough to distinguish inheritance from generic influence or after-the-fact matching.

The current SI System Model therefore treats these as distinct:

- parent local identity;
- child/coupled local and embedded identity;
- parent-child coupling geometry;
- carrier/subspace correspondence;
- participation;
- observability;
- identifiability;
- uncertainty;
- higher-order source interaction;
- hierarchical closure;
- recovery or transient response where native dynamics permit it.

A scalar chi is not required for any experiment below.

## Experimental game plan E1: adsorbate-on-substrate vibrational inheritance

### Scientific question

Can independently measured or computed clean-substrate vibrational carrier structure prospectively predict which substrate-resolved structures survive, transform, split, or hybridize after a chemically adsorbed child system is formed, beyond what is predicted by frequencies alone?

### Native objects

- clean-substrate vibrational modes/subspaces;
- adsorbate-plus-substrate coupled modes/subspaces;
- atom-resolved or layer-resolved participation;
- parent-child coupling geometry;
- frequency-resolved response or spectral density where measured;
- independent structural geometry and isotope/coverage condition.

### Proposed sequence

1. Characterize the clean substrate before the coupled target result is opened.
2. Freeze the shared-coordinate/atom mapping and correspondence rule.
3. Predict carrier-resolved descendants or subspaces in the adsorbate-coupled system.
4. Measure or compute the coupled system independently.
5. Perturb one parent property without defining the mapping from the child outcome, for example isotope, surface-layer mass, adsorbate identity, coverage, or a physically controlled substrate modification.
6. Test whether the predicted carrier transformation changes in the specified direction.
7. Compare against same-spectrum/frequency-only matching and coupling-rewire or generic-parent controls where physically meaningful.

### Outcomes that would count against SI-specific inheritance

- frequency-only or generic local mode matching performs equally well;
- the frozen parent-to-child carrier mapping does not predict the coupled system better than an appropriate scrambled/generic control;
- observed child structure changes independently of the predicted parent perturbation;
- no stable carrier/subspace relation remains after uncertainty and degeneracy are handled;
- the result depends on post-target coordinate selection.

### Capability note

This experiment may be computationally realizable using first-principles or validated force-constant data even if direct surface spectroscopy is not locally available. A literature collision must determine whether suitable existing paired clean/coupled datasets already answer the question before new fabrication or measurement is considered.

## Experimental game plan E2: thickness/depth inheritance and hierarchical closure

### Scientific question

How much parent substrate must be retained before the child/coupled response relevant to a declared higher-level question becomes invariant to additional parent depth, and does the resulting reduced subsystem remain valid when reused as a component in a larger construction?

### Native objects

- finite-depth and converged/semi-infinite response;
- self-energy/embedding or transfer response;
- carrier/subspace structure;
- reduction error as a function of retained depth;
- higher-level response after the reduced subsystem is embedded again.

### Proposed sequence

1. Define the higher-level response quantity before inspecting the depth sweep.
2. Compute or measure a sequence of increasing parent depths.
3. Establish the supported interior where additional depth does not materially alter the declared quantity within a stated numerical/measurement uncertainty.
4. Reuse the reduced subsystem at a higher level.
5. Compare the higher-level result against the unreduced reference.
6. Report `SUPPORTED`, `PARTIAL`, `FAILED`, or `UNRESOLVED` hierarchical closure by quantity rather than with one aggregate score.

### Outcomes that would count against closure

- a reduction that looks converged locally fails to preserve the higher-level response;
- different higher-level questions require incompatible retained depths;
- apparent closure depends on one favorable frequency or coordinate chosen after inspection;
- uncertainty is larger than the claimed reduction error.

## Experimental game plan E3: controlled coupled-oscillator physical analogue

### Scientific question

Can a tabletop or benchtop coupled-oscillator array reproduce the measurement semantics required by SI, including independently characterized parents, frozen carrier mapping, coupling-specific transformation, splitting/mixing, hierarchical reduction, and recovery versus transient response?

### Purpose

This is a method/measurement qualification experiment only. It cannot validate chemical, biological, geological, quantum, or materials-specific inheritance by analogy.

### Proposed system

A reconfigurable set of coupled mechanical oscillators with independently measurable masses, restoring constants, damping, coupling topology, and motion tracking.

### Manipulations

- preserve local oscillator properties while rewiring coupling;
- preserve approximate spectra while altering carrier geometry;
- vary coupling strength across an ordinary functioning range;
- introduce near-degeneracy to test subspace handling;
- group a coupled pair into an effective subsystem and re-embed it;
- perturb one parent and measure recovery/transient propagation.

### Measurements

- local and global modes;
- participation;
- carrier overlap/subspace angles;
- transfer response;
- transient amplification;
- asymptotic return;
- reduction error after grouping.

### Negative result value

A failure to recover the planted relationship under controlled hardware would be method evidence against the current measurement architecture and should be fixed or retained before any harder physical system is interpreted.

## Experimental game plan E4: multi-parent source attribution

### Scientific question

When a child system is influenced by more than one physically separable parent/source, can interventions identify main and interaction contributions without forcing them into percentages that sum to one?

### Proposed design

Use a system where source A and source B can each be independently present/absent or perturbed, so the empty, A-only, B-only, and A+B states are physically meaningful. For more sources, exact higher-order decomposition is attempted only if the full subset design is feasible.

### Key requirement

The response quantity, units, geometry, and intervention protocol are frozen before interpreting the combined condition.

### Failure modes

- source interventions cannot be independently realized;
- response definitions change across subsets;
- interaction terms are dominated by measurement drift or uncontrolled context;
- a normalized percentage decomposition is required to make the interpretation look simple.

## Experimental game plan E5: natural extreme as a limit probe

### Scientific question

Can a naturally occurring or experimentally accessible extreme coupling, near-degeneracy, strong hybridization, or thin/thick substrate state expose a failure boundary that is difficult to resolve in ordinary conditions?

### Proportionality rule

The extreme is used only as a limit probe. It does not become the representative center of SI evidence because it is rare or visually dramatic. Ordinary functioning states remain required for the Function Map.

## Pre-literature priority

The highest-priority collision questions are:

1. Have paired clean-substrate and adsorbate-coupled vibrational experiments already tested carrier-resolved parent-to-child mode inheritance rather than only mode assignment or frequency shifts?
2. Have surface Green-function/embedding studies already tested the specific hierarchical-closure question of whether a converged reduced substrate response remains adequate after re-embedding into a larger dynamical question?
3. What established experimental or computational methods are strongest for tracking mode/subspace identity across adsorption, coupling, or structural transformation?
4. What prior work already separates generic influence/hybridization from prospectively predictive carrier inheritance using perturbation or counterfactual controls?
5. Are there existing public datasets or benchmark systems that can serve as known-truth or P0-Q qualification before any new physical experiment?
