`delta = gamma/2-kappa`,

## Scientific interpretation

The broad H2 failure is now mechanistically understood in a preregistered fresh regime.

Same-record conditioning contributes two competing effects on the low-kappa overdamped branch:

1. additional direct contraction through the diagonal conditioning term;
2. a state-orientation-dependent change in reversible cross-coupling through the `2 q x z` term.

When the coupling change reduces mixing between the slower and faster-damped active directions strongly enough, the dominant decay rate can become less negative even though the system remains stable. The sign condition is captured by the registered `S` expression in the weak-measurement limit.

This explains a large structured subset of the 441 H2 counterexamples without changing or rescuing the failed H2 claim.

## Relation to the Stability Arc boundary

The mechanism explicitly contains

`sqrt(delta^2-4 omega^2)`,

so the deterministic active repeated-root boundary `delta=2 omega` enters the perturbative response geometry. However, repeated-root location alone is not sufficient: the state-orientation factor `x*z`, measurement strength `q`, and damping asymmetry also enter.

Therefore the current result supports a multicoordinate boundary picture rather than a universal one-number classifier.

## What remains open

The broad H2 stress showed no physical mean-square `STABLE -> record UNSTABLE` transitions and 584 `UNSTABLE -> STABLE` transitions. That class-monotonicity pattern was post-hoc and is not established.

The next justified target is an adversarial fresh test, and if possible a symbolic sufficient-condition analysis, of whether same-record conditioning can cross a mean-square stability boundary in the destabilizing direction within this exact measured-qubit family.
