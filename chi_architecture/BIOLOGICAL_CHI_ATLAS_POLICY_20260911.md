# Biological Chi and Atlas Plotting Policy — 2026-09-11

**Status:** P0-D governance clarification  
**Scope:** GRI / biological-domain Chi development and later Atlas use  
**Authority:** General Cross-Project Research Protocol v0.7.1 FINAL + v0.7.1A

## Principle

The current absence of an admitted biological scalar `chi` is a scientifically valuable state and must remain explicit. It prevents historical or convenient scalarizations from being mistaken for a natively derived biological stability coordinate.

At the same time, discovering a legitimate biological `chi` would be highly useful because a scalar coordinate would simplify within-domain and later cross-domain Atlas placement.

Those two statements are compatible.

## Epistemic firewall

`Atlas convenience is not evidence for Chi admission.`

The fact that a scalar would make plotting, comparison, visualization, interpolation, or cross-domain registration easier may motivate a search for a biological coordinate, but it may not determine the formula, grouping level, parameterization, sign convention, normalization, data subset, or acceptance rule.

Required direction:

`native biological dynamics -> independently justified coordinate candidate -> qualification -> biological chi if earned -> Atlas placement`

Forbidden direction:

`desired Atlas geometry or plotting convenience -> choose/tune biological chi -> claim the resulting map as biological evidence`

## Preferred outcome hierarchy

The research program should actively test for the simplest adequate coordinate without privileging simplicity over validity:

1. `ADMISSIBLE_SCALAR_BIOLOGICAL_CHI` if a one-dimensional coordinate is natively derived, identifiable, uncertainty-qualified, and adequate for the declared biological task;
2. `ADMISSIBLE_MULTIDIMENSIONAL_BIOLOGICAL_CHI_ARCHITECTURE` if multiple coordinates, lineages, modes, coupling terms, or feedback objects are required;
3. `CHI_COORDINATE_NONIDENTIFIABLE` if the model admits the coordinate but current data cannot resolve it adequately;
4. `MODEL_CLASS_INADEQUATE` if the biological dynamics are organized but the current mathematical model cannot adjudicate the coordinate;
5. `NO_PHYSICAL_BIOLOGICAL_CHI_COORDINATE` if an adequate model and adequate observations still provide no justified biological Chi role.

No lower item may be promoted upward merely to make the Atlas easier to plot.

## Why a valid biological scalar would matter

If earned independently, a biological scalar `chi_bio` would provide:

- a compact coordinate for plotting Function and Limit maps;
- direct longitudinal trajectories through state space;
- simpler uncertainty intervals around placement;
- easier comparison of perturbation, recovery, transition, and rare-limit states;
- a clean candidate coordinate for later cross-domain registration;
- a more interpretable test of whether biological systems cluster, traverse, or avoid particular regions.

These are downstream scientific and engineering advantages. They are not admission criteria.

## Search policy

The program should therefore **look for biological Chi deliberately but not force it**.

Candidate coordinates should be generated from native biological structure such as perturbation-response dynamics, recovery/persistence, dynamical lineages, feedback/coupling organization, independently characterized state transitions, or another domain-native mechanism. Historical GRI `CV/2` remains comparator/history unless it independently re-emerges from such a model.

Any candidate discovered after inspecting a dataset carries promotion debt. It may guide P0-D development but requires a separately frozen qualification design and untouched evidence before confirmatory use.

## Atlas fallback

If no scalar biological Chi earns admission, the Atlas remains viable. Biological placement can use a multidimensional coordinate chart, with scalar projections used only as labeled projections rather than as the biological Chi itself.

Thus:

`no biological scalar chi != no biological Atlas`

and

`valid biological scalar chi -> easier Atlas, not more valid Atlas by itself`.
