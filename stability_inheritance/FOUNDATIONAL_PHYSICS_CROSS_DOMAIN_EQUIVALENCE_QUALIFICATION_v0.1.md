## Core rule

Two quantities from different domains are not treated as the same class of \(\chi\), \(\Chi\), or \(\Chi_{\mathrm{arc}}\) merely because they are dimensionless, share a numerical value, or contain analogous symbols.

Cross-domain equivalence must be earned from the native dynamics.

## Equivalence levels

### E0 — Notational resemblance

Examples:
- two ratios look like rate/frequency;
- both are dimensionless;
- both happen to approach 1;
- both use words such as damping, stability, criticality, width, or transition.

Scientific status: NO EQUIVALENCE.

### E1 — Algebraic resemblance

The native equations can be rearranged into superficially similar scalar expressions, but the generators, state variables, or physical meanings differ.

Examples include:
- a resonance width-to-mass ratio versus a time-domain damped oscillator;
- a cosmological balance identity versus a second-order stable oscillator.

Scientific status: ANALOGY / COMPARISON ONLY.

### E2 — Generator-class equivalence

Required conditions:
1. the native evolution reduces to the same dynamical order and canonical generator class under a declared invertible transformation;
2. the damping-like coefficient multiplies the same derivative/generator structure;
3. the oscillatory/restoring term has the same dynamical role;
4. the discriminant or characteristic polynomial has the same repeated-root boundary;
5. the source/observable mapping does not create the equivalence artificially;
6. sign conventions and stability sectors match;
7. the proposed \(\chi\) is invariant under the permitted representation changes for the declared task.

Scientific status: SAME SCALAR STABILITY CLASS may be admitted.

A shared numerical \(\chi=1\) is meaningful across domains only at E2 or above.

### E3 — Modal/vector equivalence

In addition to E2 or an independently native modal construction:
1. modes/eigenspaces are scientifically identifiable;
2. eigenvalue/eigenvector or invariant-subspace correspondence is defined;
3. degeneracy and basis/gauge freedom are handled explicitly;
4. mode participation and coupling can be compared without label leakage;
5. modal changes predict or organize native response in both domains.

Scientific status: a cross-domain \(\Chi\) relation may be compared structurally.

E3 does not imply common microscopic mechanism.

### E4 — System/conglomerate equivalence

Required:
1. independently justified \(\Chi_{\mathrm{arc}}\) objects exist in both systems;
2. coupling topology / constraints / history / system response are preserved under the proposed mapping;
3. the mapping adds information beyond each domain's native model;
4. negative or scrambled mappings fail prospectively;
5. the relation transports to untouched evidence.

Scientific status: system-level architectural equivalence may be admitted.

### E5 — Physical inheritance / lineage

Required beyond E4:
1. a physical carrier, coupling, transfer, or causal path links source and descendant;
2. source state is measured/defined before descendant outcome;
3. intervention or counterfactual specificity is available where scientifically feasible;
4. descendant behavior changes as prospectively predicted when the source/carrier is changed;
5. native theory without the inheritance relation is insufficient for the declared task.

Scientific status: Stability Inheritance may be admitted for the specific path.

## Representation-specific tests

### Scalar \(\chi\)

To call two cross-domain scalars the same \(\chi\) class:
- E2 is the minimum.
- Width/mass, growth/expansion, decay/frequency, or barrier ratios remain E0-E1 unless the native generator passes E2.

### Modal/vector \(\Chi\)

A modal comparison may exist without scalar equivalence.

For example, two systems can both have informative eigenspaces while one refuses scalar \(\chi\). Therefore \(\Chi\) admission does not require E2 scalar equivalence.

Cross-domain modal equivalence requires E3 on the modal objects themselves.

### Conglomerate \(\Chi_{\mathrm{arc}}\)

\(\Chi_{\mathrm{arc}}\) is never inherited from scalar or modal equivalence automatically.

It requires independent system-level construction and E4 for cross-domain equivalence.

## Refusal rule

When a proposed cross-domain equivalence fails at level \(E_n\), preserve the highest passed level and stop promotion.

Examples:
- E1 passed, E2 failed: useful mathematical analogy, no common scalar class.
- E2 passed, E3 failed: common reduced scalar boundary, no common modal architecture.
- E3 passed, E4 failed: modal structural correspondence, no system-level equivalence.
- E4 passed, E5 failed: architecture correspondence without physical inheritance.

A failed higher level does not erase lower-level descriptive truth.

## Claim discipline

This qualification prevents the unified investigation from becoming a search for repeated \(\chi\approx1\) values.

The stronger question is whether native dynamical structure survives increasingly demanding equivalence tests and whether any surviving relation performs useful scientific work beyond existing theories.
